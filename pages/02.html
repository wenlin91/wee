How to Choose Topics for AI-Written Blogs?

1. Explore AI Expansion Themes and Get Inspired

Most blog creators get stuck at the beginning, with the main issue being topic selection. Initially, their minds go blank and after several tries, they can only come up with a few tired topics. Here, AI can play a unique role. While it can't replace a creator's personal life insight, it acts like a prism, breaking one beam of light into seven colours, sparking imagination.

In practice, this means entering a rough prompt into the AI, such as "dinner." AI doesn't need to produce fully finished content, just to generate limitless ideas: the digestive process of dinner, the connection between dinner and family relationships, differences in dinner among various professionals, and the physical impact of overeating... Output four to ten different perspectives at once. Of this batch, 70% won’t have real creative value, but within the remaining 30%, one or two gems may appear, opening the creator’s sealed memory drawer.

In past practice, AI has sometimes generated a quirky perspective, like asking if the sense of ritual at dinner is deliberate, instantly triggering the creator's memory. To complete social media check-ins, AI has even staged a dining table for photos and then ordered takeout to fill up. These unique details can quickly turn into a vivid essay on everyday life. AI-generated content has never been the final answer, but a spark for creativity. AI handles all the trigger points, and the creator steps on the landmine that can ignite inspiration. Without AI casting a wide net, creators can only rely on their own instincts and may never find the shore to dock at.

2. Filter high-value topics to boost self-awareness

When inspiration strikes, ideas will pop up all at once. Creators often have more than twenty potential topics ready, enough for a blog post, but they only have the time to complete one. A common mistake here is picking topics based on gut feelings, choosing ones that seem appealing at first glance, which usually ends up with content that gets a lukewarm reception.

To pick high-value topics, AI can act as a companion chat judge. You can input three to five candidate titles and have them evaluated across three solid criteria. First, know your target audience and check if it’s clear and specific enough. Second, figure out which topics really click with users’ needs, see if the content hits a common itch or a key pain point, and gauge if readers respond with a simple “oh” or a more engaged “hey.” Third, think about content retention: will readers still remember the key points clearly three minutes after reading the article?

In practice, the creators have wavered between the theme 'Weekend Park Walk Guide' and 'Three Conversations Heard on a Park Bench.' However, the evaluation results generated by AI are pretty clear: the first is general information content, with lots of similar and repetitive stuff you can find online, while the second is unique story content, which can only be put together after visiting the site and collecting material, making it irreplaceable. Based on this, they went for the second one, and after it came out, it got loads of reader comments. Many readers said the content brought back exclusive memories of just sitting in the park.

Choosing high-value themes and boosting self-awareness go hand in hand. The idea is to get creators to focus on the important stuff, spending their limited time and energy on topics that really matter. Every time the selection process is done, it reaffirms the value of their own work. By using this approach over the long term, creators can easily spot purely popular topics and make work that truly reflects their own life experiences.

3. Use expert roles to get a handle on the thematic direction

A broad look at topics from one angle shows no obvious problems, but if the main direction shifts even a bit, the creative process slowly gets stuck. For example, the initial goal is 'workplace communication,' but the content ends up turning into pointless 'workplace complaints'; the initial goal is 'sharing experiences of living alone,' but the final content deliberately drifts into 'selling loneliness.' Once the core direction is off, the overall tone of the whole content completely falls apart.

In this scenario, a targeted validation system can be used, with the instruction AI taking on three different roles to complete a topic review. The first role is a professional editor, whose main job is to spot logical gaps and unclear points in the topic. The second role is the account's loyal long-time readers, who specifically check the relevance between the content and the creator, confirming the creator's key support for conveying the theme. The third character is a completely unfamiliar ordinary passerby, judging solely by the title, making sure the title is easy to understand and accessible.

In practice, the author once prepared an article called "The Attention Crisis of Modern Society," initially coming up with a title in an academic style. The role of AI in editing was considered too broad, with creators not having authoritative data to back up the content; long-time readers noted that the topic overlapped a lot with previously published content on "mobile phone usage habits"; ordinary people said that audiences couldn't grasp the specific angle of the "attention crisis." After checking across three areas, the topic was finally changed to "The Three Most Time-Taking Apps on My Phone," with the focus directly on specific events and scenarios, making the rest of the creation process smooth and hassle-free.

Using multi-character settings doesn't mean AI is replacing creators as content experts, but rather using AI to switch perspectives and expand topics from a floating idea into something tangible. The more specific the angle, the more solid the main direction, and the less likely the creative process will wander off. Every role switch is a precise adjustment of direction. This adjustment isn't just about the topic itself, but also about the creator's deeper intentions, the target audience, and the cognitive value readers are looking for.

4. During the content system construction stage, self-question bank planning must be done at the same time.

No matter how good individual content is, it won't create a proper content lineup. If a reader accidentally clicks on a single post and closes the page after getting interested, they might easily forget about the main account later. Blogs with high subscription rates and meme-chasing tendencies all have a hidden content map behind them. By going through the content, readers can get a feel for the account's long-term creative direction and anticipate the general focus of future content.

AI can act as an efficient draftsman in this process. First, clarify the annual key creative direction, such as "Daily Awareness After Thirty." AI completes hierarchical breakdowns, which can be divided into multiple primary sections covering daily dining, living spaces, disposal of old items, intergenerational dialogue, and bodily changes. Each level continues to decompose two to three layers downwards, forming a complete tree-like structure. Then, the specific creative topics are attached to each branch to generate a question bank covering a six-month to one-year cycle.

AI can spot content gaps that creators might not even realise exist. For instance, by comparing data, the system can see that in spatial organisation content, topics related to digital spaces aren’t covered, so it misses detailed scenarios like phone albums, computer folders, or WeChat favourites, leaving clear content gaps. Filling in this content often results in even better reach than expected. By using a full question bank map, creators stop waiting passively for inspiration and start actively managing their knowledge areas. They get a clear sense of the creative flow for each section, knowing which parts to prep and which to leave for later. This solid sense of control is more valuable in the long run than the viral success of a single post.

Writing down these four subheadings actually forms a complete closed loop: first, rely on AI to open your mind, then use it to expand understanding, then build a long-term framework, and finally use multiple identities to fill in the gaps. From start to finish, AI is an assistant; the real decision-making, emotional engagement, and judgement are always made by you. AI is like a handy map—the path is yours to walk, but it lets you glance at navigation at intersections and stay steady when going uphill. With this map, every mountain you climb has a clearer direction and lighter steps.
