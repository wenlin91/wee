What impact does AI have on our lives?

👤Author：Alini📅Date：18/08/2026⏱Reading time：10-20mins

Nowadays, many people say "AI is going to replace humans," while others think, "AI is just an advanced chatbot, no big deal." Looking back in 2026, AI is no longer just a lab concept; it's like a quietly mixed cup of everyday warm water, and before you know it, it's already bringing a whole new flavour into our lives. Today, let's chat about what AI has brought to our lives from three of the most practical angles.

In daily life, we used to think AI was just a sci-fi scene from a film about flying and burrowing, but now it's long since crept into the little details of every meal and season. Some people wake up in the morning, and the smart speaker sets the air conditioner to a comfy 26 degrees based on their sleep data from the day before, and prepares their favourite soy milk in advance. Some people's navigation before heading out no longer just shows a congested route; it takes into account real-time traffic light durations, today's market crowd, and even whether their favourite breakfast shop is open today, planning the best route to grab fried dough sticks on the way without having to wait an extra three minutes. Even older family members can benefit from AI: relatives used to spend half an hour registering on their phones, but now they just tell AI, "Help me book an eye clinic appointment on Wednesday at the nearest hospital to my home," and it's done in ten seconds. In the past, we always said, "You have to take control of your own life." Now, AI is more like a thoughtful life assistant. It doesn’t take your steering wheel; it just helps you move the small stones ahead of time, freeing up time wasted on trivial matters to spend with family, take walks, and do things that truly make you happy. Of course, there are some minor annoyances, like AI constantly guessing what you like when scrolling through short videos, sometimes scrolling late into the night. But overall, what it adds to daily life isn’t "magic"—it’s genuinely worry-free.

When it comes to work and money, a lot of people’s first thought is that AI is taking jobs, but really it’s more like AI is giving jobs a new label. A friend who does copywriting used to spend three days researching and revising product intros five times. Now, with AI, he comes up with three different first drafts in various styles. He just spends an hour tweaking the one that fits the brand best, and the rest of the time he can focus on more important brand planning. Last month, he even got a raise for being more efficient. Experienced factory technicians used to have to monitor machine settings round the clock, but now AI can predict problems before they happen. He no longer needs to stay up late on shift, becoming an 'AI master' teaching AI to spot special working situations, and earning more than before. On a bigger scale, AI has led to loads of new jobs that didn’t exist before: AI prompt engineer, AI trainer, AI content moderator. Last year, over two million AI-related positions were created. Before, small businesses spent hundreds of yuan hiring designers for event posters. Now, with AI, they can churn out a dozen pages in just a few minutes at almost no cost. Tons of small fruit stall or bubble tea shop owners use AI-made posters to bring in customers, and business has really picked up. AI isn’t taking jobs away; it just handles the repetitive, boring, and tough physical stuff, leaving us to do more creative, human-touch work, which makes the whole economy run smoother than before.

From a social and ethical perspective, AI is moving too fast, and new problems we've never faced before are now out in the open. For instance, in the past, when you took a photo and posted it on Moments, no one could easily turn it into a video. But now, AI can create a video of you talking in a place you've never even been, based on a static photo. If someone with bad intentions tricks people, ordinary folks can't even tell what's real and what's fake. There's also the issue of the algorithm's 'favouritism': whatever content you scroll through, AI always pushes more of it to you. Over time, you start thinking the whole world is just like you see it, and you slowly end up living in an information bubble. But on the flip side, AI is also helping us fill many gaps that society couldn't cover before: now, many places use AI to help lost elderly people find their way home. Before, they had to rely on community notices, but now uploading photos can match clues within hours. Last year, over ten thousand missing elderly people across the country were found thanks to AI; AI also offers customised tutoring for children in remote mountainous areas. Previously, there weren’t enough teachers in the mountains, but now AI can adjust the pace of lessons to each child's learning speed, letting kids in the mountains enjoy educational resources similar to those in the city. What we need to do now isn’t shut down AI, but quickly set clear rules: what AI absolutely cannot do, which data must not be used arbitrarily, just like we set traffic rules for cars decades ago—not to ban cars, but to let them run safely.

In fact, AI has never been a 'rival' to replace humans; it's more like a new hoe we've built with our own hands. If you use it to cultivate the land properly, it'll help you harvest more grain; but if you use it for dodgy purposes, it'll naturally harm you. Although it's a bit magical, it's not that scary. It's just a mirror showing the kind of life we want and pushing us to think about how to use it. After all, it's never the algorithms that decide where AI ultimately goes, but us holding the mouse.
