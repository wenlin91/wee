A Practical Discussion on AI-Driven Blogging

👤 Author: Alini📅 Date: 18/08/2026⏱ Reading time: 10-20 mins

I once saw readers' comments and read several articles about AI blogging. I understood the principles, but when I actually sat down and switched on my computer, I still didn't know where to click first. I thought about it, and it's true. No matter how good the methodology is, if it doesn't take root, it's just a castle in the air. So in this blog, I'll fully share my insights from writing blogs with AI over the past six months, explaining each step step by step. I don't mess around with exaggerated theory; every step is a habit I've repeatedly tried, corrected, and finally settled on.

1. Preparation Phase: Clarify Goals and Materials

When switching on AI, don't rush to type. I've been there before—I asked AI to "write a blog about time management" straight away, only to end up with a load of silly nonsense, wasting both time and energy.

During the preparation phase, do two things: first, think through three questions. Who is the article for? What have readers been concerned about lately? After reading the article, will readers relate? For example, the 'early rising' piece written for office workers worries readers about the alarm not going off, but what I hope readers can take away is 'Don't force yourself to get up at five; you can still make it to work by 6:30.' Once you understand these three questions, the goal becomes clear.

Second, dig up the 'notes' in your mind. Even if it's just some vague memories, a chat record, a casual photo, or a little story that happened yesterday, all of it can be piled into blank documents. These are the materials you use to set prompts; no matter how good AI is, it can't come up with details you've actually experienced.

With these preparations, switch on the AI. At this point, the text you type is like going to the AI with a gift, and the AI will generously reward your genuine knowledge. In other words, you need half a bowl of water before AI can help you fill the whole bowl.

2. Generate Outlines and Build Logical Frameworks and Structures

Once you have the material and your goals are clear, don't rush to let AI write the main text. Otherwise, articles that are rushed often end up with a loose structure and messy layers, and revising them is basically rewriting.

My approach is: first, feed the AI the materials organised during the preparation phase, then let it do just one thing—write the outline. You say: "Based on these materials, generate three different article structures." The first is the story outline; the second is the problem breakdown list; the third is the key points of the experience summary. Each structure should have a clear beginning, main body, and conclusion, with the main part further split into two to four sections.

AI can usually produce three framework outlines in just a few dozen seconds. But my work was only just beginning, so I had to lay these three frameworks side by side and compare them one by one. Think about which opening hook is the most accurate, which main section has the smoothest flow, and which ending is the strongest. Sometimes I choose one to tweak, sometimes I pull a bit from each of the two to make a new one.

This process is a lot like building a house: first, you look at the design plans, and only after you're happy with the plans do you start building the walls. Plenty of examples and checks can save you a couple of hours of revision time during this step.

3. Generate content using segmented filling and iteration

Once the outline is set, you can move on to the content generation stage. But here's a handy tip: don’t let AI generate the whole article at once. The longer the content it produces, the more likely it is to go off topic, which reduces your focus and the overall quality of the article.

I break down each blog post into several 'block units'—a separate section at the beginning, a block under each subheading in the main body, and a separate section at the end. Then I fill them in one by one. For example, when writing the introduction, I give the AI a clear prompt: 'Use the story in the material as an introduction, keep it under 180 words, use a slightly humorous tone, and finally form a hook with a question.' After the AI has generated it, I check and make sure it meets the requirements, then move on to the next block. If you feel like you've strayed from the topic, immediately tweak it and adjust your prompt to start again.

The biggest advantage of this iterative approach of "fill in one area, look at a paragraph, adjust one piece" is that every step is under your control. It's like a puzzle, placing only one piece at a time, making sure it fits perfectly before adding the next. With this approach, at least you won't hit a point of collapse halfway through writing, only to find the previous content drifting away from what needs to be done. And every time a block is generated, you get the chance to pop in something of your own: a specific number, a real conversation, or a mental activity at that moment. These "personal touches" are like nails, firmly embedding AI-written content into your own experiential thinking, impossible to pull out.

4. Optimise and Polish to Improve Quality

After finishing the first draft, the manuscript often still shows clear machine-like features. The sentences are neatly structured, paragraphs are evenly sized, and the overall reading feels almost robotic, lacking a natural expressive touch. This is when you can move on to the focused polishing stage.

The polishing work happens in two steps, without changing the main idea or the established facts. The first step is AI-assisted fat reduction, which follows three simple rules. First, split all long sentences over thirty words into shorter ones. Second, change all passive voice sentences into active voice. Third, remove extra adverbs before adjectives, like changing "very good" to "good." The main goal of this step is to get rid of any unnecessary fluff from AI-generated content, keeping the text clean and tidy.

The second level is hands-on muscle-building tasks, which have to be done personally by the creator. The main idea is to spot the parts of the manuscript that are "logically correct but completely lacking warmth" and swap them for unique, personal expressions that show your own experiences. For instance, the AI-generated "This method is very practical" could become "I've tried this method for a month, and I ended up buying three fewer rolls of kitchen towel." The AI-generated "persistence will yield rewards" could be changed to "I kept going until the twenty-first day, and I actually started looking forward to that moment."

Each retouching should set some acceptable standards. When reading the full text, the sentences should feel as natural as chatting with friends, which is fine. But if it only seems appropriate but doesn't match everyday casual phrases, it’s simply removed. All the content that remains should be understandable for you, and readers should also be able to understand it.

5. Follow-up review, manual finishing

During the final stage of polishing the manuscript, there's a key step you can't skip: reading through the whole text. Read the polished manuscript out loud from start to finish, holding a marker as you go. If you come across any content that feels awkward, off-paced or uncertain, mark it straight away and make a note of it.

The reading section can get rid of the lack of expression in AI writing. Sentences that look fine on paper but feel odd when read aloud, and expressions that make sense logically but feel stiff, become really clear when spoken. Usually, when going through the fourth and fifth paragraphs, AI tends to repeat stuff, which is then completely cut out and replaced with genuine original content.

At the final stage of the audit process, three checks can be set. First, all the facts in the manuscript need to be verified one by one. Second, the main direction of the manuscript must match the initial goals completely. Third, after regular readers finish the page, they should be able to remember at least one sentence with useful information. If any of these checks fail, it means the article is lacking and should go back to the prep stage for more adjustments and improvements.

After completing these five steps, you'll see that AI has never actually created any text of its own for you. It's just a super keen helper—laying paper, grinding ink, and mixing colours—but every stroke you make is your own wrist at work. This is the best example of human-machine teamwork: the machine doesn't snatch your pen, it just helps you get stuck in—you're the boss of your own writing!
